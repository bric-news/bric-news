title: "PUCO Weighs How to Regulate Apartment Submetering After Court Ruling"
summary: "The Public Utilities Commission of Ohio is deciding how to regulate Nationwide Energy Partners, a company that buys electricity wholesale and resells it to residents of Ohio apartment complexes, after the Ohio Supreme Court ruled in May that NEP operates as a utility under state law. AEP Ohio argues NEP is illegally reselling power inside AEP's exclusive service territory without PUCO approval; NEP argues the law's definitions do not clearly cover its submetering model."
detail: "The dispute traces to a May Ohio Supreme Court ruling that rejected PUCO's earlier finding that submetering companies were not utilities because landlords, not the submetering company, technically supplied the power. Justice Patrick DeWine wrote that Nationwide Energy Partners, which buys electricity wholesale and resells it at retail rates to residents behind an apartment complex's master meter, is clearly in the business of supplying electricity regardless of the landlord's role. The court returned the case to PUCO with instructions to treat NEP as a utility while resolving two related AEP Ohio complaints.\n\nAEP argues Ohio's Certified Territories Act gives incumbent utilities exclusive rights to sell power in their territory, and NEP operated there without PUCO approval. NEP counters that the law's definitions of \"electric load center\" and \"distribution lines\" do not clearly cover a company selling power behind a complex's master meter rather than owning distribution lines itself.\n\nThe Ohio Consumers' Counsel says PUCO previously ordered AEP to let five apartment complexes, about 1,171 units, convert to NEP's submetered billing. Of those residents, 510 had used a competitive power supplier, 56 were on an AEP budget billing plan, and three used Ohio's Percent of Income Payment Plan, all lost under NEP's billing. The Counsel wants PUCO to require NEP's customers keep the same rate protections and payment plans as other utility customers, regardless of NEP's final legal classification. Gov. Mike DeWine vetoed separate submetering legislation in June, calling the business model itself flawed."
why_it_matters: "Any Columbus landlord using or considering a submetering arrangement like NEP's for utility billing should watch this docket, since PUCO's decision on required consumer protections could set new terms, such as payment plan access, that a submetering vendor would have to build into its contract with the property."
