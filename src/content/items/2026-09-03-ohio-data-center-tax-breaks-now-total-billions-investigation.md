title: "Ohio Data Center Tax Breaks Now Total Billions, Investigation Finds"
summary: "A Dayton Daily News investigation found Ohio's state and local governments have granted billions of dollars in tax breaks to attract data centers, including decades-old state agreements with Amazon, Google and Meta that waive sales tax on building materials for as many data centers as those companies build in Ohio. Local cities and townships have layered on additional, often decades-long property tax abatements. Ohio now hosts roughly 240 data centers, the fifth-highest total of any state, even as residents in several communities have pushed back on the projects."
detail: "The investigation found that most Ohio data center projects qualify for state sales tax exemptions on building materials under agreements the state struck with Amazon, Google and Meta in the 2010s, deals Ohio cannot get out of for decades despite the exemptions costing billions more in forgone state revenue than originally projected. On top of the state exemption, local city and township governments have added their own property tax abatements, in many cases lasting decades, even as residents in communities hosting or considering these projects have voiced opposition. Ohio is now home to roughly 240 data centers, the fifth-highest total among U.S. states, the newspaper found. The abatements mean that as data center construction expands across the state, much of the property tax revenue that would normally flow to local schools and governments from that new construction is instead waived for years or decades, shifting the tax base and drawing scrutiny from residents and some state lawmakers over how property tax policy is being used to court large employers."
why_it_matters: "Local property tax abatements for data centers reduce the tax base that funds schools and services in the communities that grant them, which is part of why several Ohio cities are now facing ballot fights and legislative proposals over how these deals get approved."
