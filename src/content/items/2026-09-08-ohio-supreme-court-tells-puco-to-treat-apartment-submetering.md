title: "Ohio Supreme Court tells PUCO to treat apartment submetering company as a utility"
summary: "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled unanimously in May that Nationwide Energy Partners, which resells electricity to residents of apartment complexes across Ohio, is operating as a utility and sent the case back to the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio. AEP Ohio argues NEP is providing unlicensed retail electric service inside AEP's exclusive territory. NEP disputes that the law's definitions apply to its submetering model."
detail: "Submetering companies like Nationwide Energy Partners buy electricity wholesale and resell it to individual apartments behind a complex's master meter, typically at retail rates the landlord does not set directly. The Ohio Supreme Court has now twice told the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio it cannot treat these companies as outside its jurisdiction, most recently in a unanimous May ruling that rejected PUCO's earlier finding that landlords, not NEP, were the ones supplying the power.\n\nThe case returned to PUCO is now a dispute over how far state utility law reaches. AEP Ohio argues NEP is providing retail electric service without PUCO authorization inside AEP's protected territory under Ohio's Certified Territories Act. NEP counters that the law's terms, including \"electric load center\" and \"distribution lines,\" were written for traditional utilities and do not describe its submetering arrangement.\n\nThe Ohio Consumers' Counsel, representing residential ratepayers, says the fight has real consequences for tenants: when AEP was ordered to convert five apartment complexes to submetered NEP service, about 1,171 residents lost access to options they'd had as AEP customers, including a competitive power provider, AEP's budget billing program, and the state's Percent of Income Payment Plan. The Consumers' Counsel is asking PUCO to require NEP, if it continues operating, to provide PUCO-approved rates, payment plans, and shutoff protections comparable to a regulated utility."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus-area landlord using or considering a submetering vendor for a multifamily property should expect PUCO to set new rate and consumer-protection rules for that arrangement, and should watch whether residents keep access to competitive suppliers and income-based payment plans once a complex switches."
