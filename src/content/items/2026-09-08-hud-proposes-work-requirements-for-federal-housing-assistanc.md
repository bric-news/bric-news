title: "HUD Proposes Work Requirements for Federal Housing Assistance"
summary: "HUD unveiled a proposed rule on Friday that would let local public housing authorities require work and set time limits for able bodied adults receiving public housing or housing choice voucher assistance, with agencies able to require up to 40 hours a week of work and cap assistance at two years or more. HUD says seniors, children and people with disabilities would be exempt. The Housing Authority of Champaign County and the Vermilion Housing Authority in Illinois are the first two agencies to join HUD's new Work and Dignity Coalition."
detail: "HUD Secretary Scott Turner announced the proposed rule Friday alongside officials from two Illinois housing authorities. It would give local public housing agencies the option, not the requirement, to impose work obligations and time limits on able bodied adults who receive public housing or housing choice voucher assistance. Agencies could require up to 40 hours a week of work activity and set assistance limits of two years or more for that population. HUD says seniors, children and people with disabilities would not be subject to the new rules.\n\nHUD cited federal data showing that nearly half of non elderly, able bodied assisted households had no working adult in 2024, and that the average length of stay in major rental assistance programs has grown to roughly eight or nine years, with about half of able bodied voucher recipients staying more than 15 years. HUD estimates the changes could add up to $51 million in income nationally if adopted broadly.\n\nThe department also launched a Work and Dignity Coalition to encourage other housing authorities to adopt similar policies; the Housing Authority of Champaign County and the Vermilion Housing Authority, both in Illinois, are the first members. The rule now enters the standard federal rulemaking process, including a public comment period, before any housing authority could put it into effect."
why_it_matters: "Local public housing authorities, including CMHA in Columbus, would decide on their own whether to adopt work requirements once the rule is final, so a landlord who leases to voucher holders here could eventually see new work documentation and tenants cycling off assistance sooner than the eight to nine year stays HUD describes as typical today."
