title: "HUD Sets One-Year Deadline for Accessibility Complaints"
summary: "HUD issued a memo on August 31, 2026 setting a one-year deadline for filing Fair Housing Act accessibility complaints against buildings of four or more units, measured from the date of the certificate of occupancy. The prior position treated an inaccessible unit as a continuing violation that could be reported at any time. HUD now says the violation ends when construction ends, so buildings that received a certificate of occupancy more than a year ago are no longer subject to a HUD complaint over design and construction defects, according to disability rights group DREDF."
detail: "The Fair Housing Act requires buildings with four or more units, built after March 13, 1991, to include accessibility features: entrances without stairs, doorways wide enough for a wheelchair, an accessible route through the unit, reachable light switches and thermostats, and bathroom walls reinforced for grab bars. Elevator buildings must meet the standard on every floor; buildings without elevators must meet it on the ground floor. In an August 31, 2026 memo, HUD changed how long someone has to file a complaint over a violation of those standards. Previously, HUD treated an inaccessible unit as an ongoing violation, so a complaint could be filed years after construction as long as the barrier still existed. HUD's new position is that the violation occurs during construction and the one-year clock starts on the date of the certificate of occupancy, the document a local government issues certifying a building ready for people to move into. Under the new rule, HUD will reject as untimely most complaints against buildings that have held a certificate of occupancy for more than a year, according to DREDF, which published an explainer of the memo. The underlying construction requirements have not changed, only the window in which HUD itself will accept a complaint about them."
why_it_matters: "Owners and buyers of newly built fourplex-or-larger rental property now have a firm one-year window after the certificate of occupancy before HUD will stop accepting an accessibility complaint over how the building was designed and built, which shortens how long that construction exposure realistically lasts."
