title: "Ohio Supreme Court Sides With Petitioners in Data Center Ballot Fight"
summary: "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled Sept. 1 that Trenton, Ohio officials unlawfully blocked a citizen petition to put a ban on new data centers to a citywide vote, and ordered the city to certify the petition and forward it to the Butler County Board of Elections. The dispute centered on how to count the 10 percent of electors needed under the Ohio Constitution to qualify a charter amendment for the ballot; the court sided with residents' lower count of 128 signatures over the city's 820. A special election must be held 60 to 120 days after certification."
detail: "The case began when Trenton residents filed a petition on July 8 with 336 valid signatures seeking a charter amendment to stop future data center development in the city, located about 38 miles north of Cincinnati. Trenton City Council rejected certification on Aug. 3, arguing the petition fell short of the Ohio Constitution's requirement that at least 10 percent of the city's electors support a charter amendment. The dispute turned on how to measure that 10 percent: residents argued it should be based on turnout in Trenton's last general municipal election, requiring 128 signatures, while the city argued it should be based on all eligible voters, requiring 820.\n\nIn a Sept. 1 decision, the Ohio Supreme Court unanimously agreed with residents, finding the council had disregarded applicable law, though the justices declined to award attorney fees. The city must now certify the petition to the Butler County Board of Elections, and because it missed the November ballot deadline, a special election on the data center ban is required within 60 to 120 days of certification. Trenton is already the site of two proposed data center projects, a 141 acre Prologis facility and an Amazon complex proposed outside city limits."
why_it_matters: "This is now the second time this year the Ohio Supreme Court has ruled that a city miscounted the signature threshold for a citizen petition, and it sets the same math other Columbus area suburbs, including Grove City's own pending data center ballot fight, would have to follow if a city tried to keep a similar measure off the ballot."
