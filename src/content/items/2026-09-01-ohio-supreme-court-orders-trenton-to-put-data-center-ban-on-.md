title: "Ohio Supreme Court Orders Trenton to Put Data Center Ban on Ballot"
summary: "The Ohio Supreme Court unanimously ruled that Trenton, near Cincinnati, must let voters decide a proposed charter amendment banning new data center construction, rejecting the city council's argument that petitioners needed signatures from all registered voters rather than those who cast ballots in the last election. Five residents gathered 336 signatures against a required 128 under that lower threshold."
detail: "The Ohio Supreme Court ordered Trenton, a Butler County city near Cincinnati, to place a proposed ban on new data center construction before voters after the city council rejected a citizen petition against it. Five residents circulated the petition seeking to amend the city charter following proposals for two, and potentially three, data centers in Trenton. The charter requires signatures from 10 percent of the city's electors. The council argued that meant 10 percent of all registered voters, requiring 820 signatures, while residents argued it meant 10 percent of those who voted in the last election, requiring 128; residents submitted 336. The justices sided unanimously with the residents, finding clear and convincing evidence the council disregarded applicable law in refusing to certify the petition. If the measure cannot be certified in time for the November ballot, the court ordered a special election within 120 days of certification, which attorneys for the residents said could still land on the November 3 ballot. This is the second data center ballot case the court has decided in a month; in August it ordered the village of Ashville to let voters decide on a data center deal with EdgeConneX after rejecting the city's use of emergency legislation to bypass a public vote."
why_it_matters: "The ruling sets the signature-count standard other Ohio cities will likely face when residents petition against a data center or other large development, giving Columbus-area landlords watching similar local zoning fights a court precedent on which side controls that threshold."
