summary: "FHFA Director Bill Pulte instructed Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to immediately let all mortgage lenders use VantageScore 4.0 as an alternative to the standard three-bureau FICO credit pull, expanding a rollout that had been limited to a small group of lenders. Pulte said FICO has raised its per-score price 1,800% since 2020 and accused the credit bureaus of acting like a cartel. The Mortgage Bankers Association separately wants to end the three-bureau pull requirement for strong-credit borrowers, while FICO says its newest score remains the most predictive option."
detail: "The Federal Housing Finance Agency has expanded lender access to VantageScore 4.0 for loans sold to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, with Director Bill Pulte instructing the agencies to approve all lenders rather than the small group in an earlier limited rollout. Pulte framed the move as breaking up what he called a credit bureau monopoly, saying FICO's per-score price has risen 1,800 percent since 2020. The Mortgage Bankers Association is separately pushing to end the requirement that lenders pull all three credit bureau reports, known as a tri-merge, arguing a single-file pull would cut costs without materially raising risk; critics say the tri-merge model catches bureau errors a single pull would miss. FICO said its newer FICO Score 10T remains the most predictive score on the market. An analysis from Keefe, Bruyette and Woods found VantageScore loan volume spiked to 4.4 percent of the total in July but remains concentrated among Rocket Mortgage and United Wholesale Mortgage. The Community Home Lenders of America welcomed the expansion as a way to increase competition and lower borrower costs."
why_it_matters: "Columbus investors financing rental purchases could eventually see lower credit-report costs or an easier qualifying path if their lender adopts VantageScore instead of the standard three-bureau pull, though most lenders are still defaulting to FICO for now."
