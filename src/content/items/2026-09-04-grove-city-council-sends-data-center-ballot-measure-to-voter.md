title: "Grove City Council Sends Data Center Ballot Measure to Voters"
summary: "Grove City Council voted unanimously Sept. 4 to put the Community Consent Amendment, a citizen petition requiring a citywide vote before any large scale development, on the Nov. 3 ballot, after rejecting a shorter compromise version negotiated with the group behind it. The measure does not name data centers directly, but grew out of resident opposition to proposed data center projects in the city. Council also passed a resolution on the record opposing the amendment, citing potential effects on churches, parks, businesses and residential development."
detail: "Grove City Council held a special meeting Sept. 4 to decide the fate of the Community Consent Amendment, or CCA, a citizen-initiated charter change that would require a citywide vote before large scale development projects could move forward. The council first considered a compromise version negotiated between Protect Grove City organizers, the city law director and some council members, which included concessions on existing development, expansion, monitoring and fines. That compromise failed before it reached a vote; several council members said the public had not seen it, even though it addressed the concerns of the roughly 1,600 residents who signed the original petition.\n\nCouncil then voted unanimously to place the original CCA on the November ballot, with members saying they had a constitutional obligation to do so once the petition met the signature threshold, despite personal reservations about the measure. Council separately passed a resolution, also unanimous, putting members on record opposing the CCA over its potential impact on churches, parks, businesses and residential development citywide.\n\nAlthough the amendment's language does not mention data centers, the petition drive was sparked by growing opposition to proposed large-scale data center developments in Grove City. The general election is Nov. 3, and both supporters and opponents of the amendment say they plan to campaign directly to residents before then."
why_it_matters: "If Grove City voters approve the Community Consent Amendment in November, any developer or investor planning a large project in the city, not just data centers, would need to clear a citywide vote first, adding a step that most Columbus suburbs do not currently require."
