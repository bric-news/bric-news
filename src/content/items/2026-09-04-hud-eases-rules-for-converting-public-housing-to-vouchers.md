title: "HUD Eases Rules for Converting Public Housing to Vouchers"
summary: "HUD updated its Section 18 rule in late August, loosening criteria that let public housing authorities convert aging public housing to a model funded by Section 8 and Tenant Protection Vouchers instead of federal operating and capital funds alone. The change widens the definition of an obsolete building eligible for demolition, disposal, or redevelopment under Section 18. HUD estimates the nation's roughly 1.2 million public housing units carry a $170 billion capital repair backlog."
detail: "Section 18 of the United States Housing Act of 1937 lets public housing authorities move units out of the traditional public housing funding stream and into a voucher-based model, where tenants use Section 8 or Tenant Protection Vouchers instead of the authority relying solely on federal operating and capital funds. In late August 2026, HUD loosened the criteria for making that switch in three ways, according to Tanya Dempsey of municipal advisory firm CSG Advisors. The most significant change expands the definition of an obsolete building, making it easier for a housing authority to file to demolish, dispose of, redevelop, or sell a property as affordable housing under Section 18. Once a property converts, landlords become eligible to rent units to tenants holding those vouchers. Eric Oberdorfer of the National Association of Housing and Redevelopment Officials said public housing authorities have struggled to maintain properties since operating costs began outpacing tenant incomes decades ago, and the update is meant to draw private capital into repairs federal funding alone has not covered. The change affects how the nation's 3,300-plus public housing authorities and roughly 1.2 million public housing households can restructure funding, and how many voucher holders enter the private rental market as conversions proceed."
why_it_matters: "As more public housing units convert to the voucher model under the eased rule, the pool of Section 8 and Tenant Protection Voucher holders looking for private rentals is likely to grow, which matters to any Columbus-area landlord who accepts or is weighing whether to accept voucher tenants."
