detail: "Property taxes and data centers have become the two dominant issues in Ohio's governor's race, and both major candidates released dueling plans this week. Republican Vivek Ramaswamy said an order he would sign on his first day in office would require new data centers to generate their own power, with excess capacity going into power purchase agreements intended to lower electric costs for nearby residents, though he clarified residents would still pay for the power itself, just not certain add-on costs. He also proposes prioritizing abandoned industrial sites over farmland for new data centers. On property taxes, Ramaswamy pledged to roll the statewide tax base back to pre-pandemic levels, which he called the largest property tax rollback in state history, while capping future growth; he did not provide a dollar estimate or say how the rollback would interact with local levies voters approved since 2021, saying details would be worked out in legislation. He also wants to reduce Ohio schools' reliance on local property taxes over time. Democratic candidate Amy Acton has proposed a moratorium on new data centers unless they cover 100 percent of utility costs, sit on brownfields, and use union labor, along with property tax relief targeted at middle-income, older and disabled homeowners through the homestead exemption and a rebate tied to bills rising more than 4 percent a year."
why_it_matters: "Whichever plan advances after November would reset the statewide property tax base Columbus-area landlords are taxed against, since both candidates are proposing changes to the base itself rather than one-time local relief."
