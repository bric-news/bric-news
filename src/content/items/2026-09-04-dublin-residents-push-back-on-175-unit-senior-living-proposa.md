title: "Dublin Residents Push Back on 175-Unit Senior Living Proposal"
summary: "Sentry Land Company's concept plan for a 175 unit senior living community and 19 villas on 17.6 acres near Bright Road and Emerald Parkway in Dublin is drawing opposition from nearby homeowners, who say the three story main building exceeds the density the area's Envision Dublin plan anticipates. City staff calculated the proposed 207,514 square foot building at about 18,000 square feet per acre on land designated for a maximum of roughly 9,500 square feet per acre."
detail: "Sentry Land Company presented its latest concept for a senior living development to the Dublin Planning and Zoning Commission last month, proposing roughly 175 senior apartments and 19 single story villas, including independent living, assisted living and memory care, on 17.6 acres near Bright Road and Emerald Parkway, next to Hopewell Elementary School. The project remains at the nonbinding concept stage and would still need to clear rezoning and both preliminary and final development plan review.\n\nCity staff found the site partly covered by a \"neighborhood office\" designation under Dublin's Envision Dublin plan, which generally caps density around 9,500 square feet per acre and calls for one to two story buildings with a residential character. Sentry's proposed 207,514 square foot main building works out to roughly 18,000 square feet per acre on that portion of the site and would rise three stories, which residents argue conflicts with the area's 2013 Bright Road Plan describing a rural residential character.\n\nNearby homeowners have raised concerns about traffic on Bright Road and Emerald Parkway, where the current concept limits one entrance to right-in, right-out turns, as well as lighting and the loss of the neighborhood's low density feel. A Change.org petition opposing the project's density and traffic configuration has drawn more than 100 signatures. Sentry cites Dublin's 2023 housing study in support of the project, though that study's more detailed projections estimate a need for 110 new senior rental units through 2030, not the larger overall housing figure Sentry's materials reference."
why_it_matters: "This is the kind of density fight that shows up whenever a project exceeds what a suburb's comprehensive plan anticipates for a site, so an investor evaluating land near Bright Road and Emerald Parkway should treat Dublin's 9,500 square foot per acre neighborhood office cap as a real constraint, at least until the rezoning stage clarifies where the city lands."
