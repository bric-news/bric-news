title: "HUD Rule Change Opens Public Housing to Private Capital"
summary: "HUD updated its Section 18 guidance in late August to make it easier for public housing authorities to bring in private financing and convert aging public housing units to the Section 8 voucher model. The change broadens what counts as a functionally obsolete building, raises the unit threshold for small agencies converting to vouchers from 50 to 75 units, and lets mixed finance properties use Section 18 once their 15 year low income housing tax credit period ends. HUD estimates the public housing system carries a $170 billion capital needs backlog."
detail: "Section 18 of the United States Housing Act of 1937 lets public housing authorities demolish, dispose of or convert public housing units so residents move to Section 8 or Tenant Protection Vouchers instead, opening the door to private developers and lenders. HUD's August update loosens the eligibility criteria in three ways, according to policy specialists interviewed by Bisnow. First, it expands the definition of a functionally obsolete building; any unit built before 1950 (at least 270,000 nationally) may now qualify if bringing it up to code would cost more than 57 to 62 percent of a full rebuild. Second, it raises the unit count threshold for small agencies eligible to convert an entire property to vouchers, from 50 units to 75. Third, it allows mixed finance properties built with low income housing tax credits to use Section 18 once their 15 year compliance period ends, addressing buildings advocates say have been stuck without funding to recapitalize.\n\nHUD estimates the nation's public housing stock has a $170 billion backlog of capital needs. The agency says the changes are meant to attract private developers, lenders and tax credit investors to properties that federal appropriations alone have not kept up."
