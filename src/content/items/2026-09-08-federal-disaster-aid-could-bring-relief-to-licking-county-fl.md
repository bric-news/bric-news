title: "Federal Disaster Aid Could Bring Relief to Licking County Flood Victims"
summary: "FEMA approved $36 million in disaster funding for Michigan, Wisconsin and Ohio two days after Gov. Mike DeWine asked President Trump for a Presidential Major Disaster Declaration covering 23 Ohio counties, including Licking County, hit by severe storms and flooding from Aug. 10 to 23. Initial FEMA and Small Business Administration damage assessments found major damage to 806 properties statewide and minor damage to 475 more, with at least 50 damaged beyond repair."
detail: "Gov. Mike DeWine sent a letter to President Trump, co-signed by Rep. Troy Balderson and Sens. Jon Husted and Bernie Moreno, requesting a Presidential Major Disaster Declaration for 23 Ohio counties, including Licking County, after severe storms and flooding from Aug. 10 to 23 caused what DeWine's letter called damage exceeding the state's own capacity to address. FEMA and Small Business Administration initial assessments found major damage to 806 properties statewide and minor damage to 475 more, with at least 50 properties damaged beyond repair.\n\nTwo days after receiving the letter, the Trump administration approved $36 million in FEMA funding for Ohio, Michigan and Wisconsin, drawn from Public Assistance, Hazard Mitigation Grant Program, and Building Resilient Infrastructure and Communities funds. Trump has not yet issued the broader major disaster declaration DeWine requested, which would unlock FEMA's Individual Assistance Grant Program, Transitional Sheltering Assistance and Small Business Administration disaster loans directly for residents needing rental assistance and home repairs.\n\nLicking County Emergency Management said Pataskala was among the hardest hit areas, along with Heath, Buckeye Lake, Hebron and parts of Newark. The county is hosting Multi-Agency Resource Center events Sept. 9 and 10 at the Hebron New Life Community Center, where legal aid and the county auditor's office will be available to help residents with property tax questions tied to the flooding."
why_it_matters: "A Licking County landlord with flood-damaged rental property should keep every repair receipt and insurance record now, since the county emergency management office says that documentation is what unlocks FEMA's individual assistance and SBA loan programs if and when Trump approves the broader disaster declaration DeWine requested."
