title: "The 30 Day Deadline Ohio Landlords Miss on Security Deposits"
summary: "RL Property Management walked through the ORC 5321.16 timeline that governs a Columbus tenant move-out. A landlord has 30 days after the rental agreement ends and possession is returned to send an itemized list of deductions along with any money owed; miss it, or deduct something unsupported, and the tenant can recover the wrongfully withheld amount plus damages and attorney's fees."
detail: "An Ohio tenant move-out runs on a single hard deadline, according to RL Property Management: ORC 5321.16(B) gives a landlord 30 days from the termination of the rental agreement and the delivery of possession, not from when repairs are finished, to send a written, itemized list of any deductions along with the remaining deposit balance. A tenant who is denied deposit money without adequate support can recover that amount, plus damages equal to the amount wrongfully withheld, plus reasonable attorney's fees.\n\nThe piece also flags a deposit interest rule the company says many owners overlook. ORC 5321.16(A) requires 5 percent annual interest on the portion of a security deposit that exceeds the greater of $50 or one month's rent, once a tenant has been in possession more than six months. Because a two month deposit already exceeds that threshold, it automatically triggers the interest requirement on any tenancy that runs past six months.\n\nOn abandoned property, RL Property Management notes Ohio has no statute prescribing how a landlord must notice, store or dispose of belongings a tenant leaves behind, though ORC 5321.15 separately prohibits an owner from seizing a tenant's possessions. The company recommends documenting the unit room by room against the original move-in report and building the turnover scope of work from that same inspection, treating the move-out inspection and the turn scope as one document rather than two separate steps.\n\nThe guidance is presented as informational, reflecting Ohio law as of September 2026, and recommends owners consult an Ohio attorney for a specific dispute."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus landlord holding a deposit larger than one month's rent on a tenant who has been in place more than six months owes 5 percent annual interest on the excess under ORC 5321.16(A), a calculation that is easy to skip and expensive to get challenged on later."
