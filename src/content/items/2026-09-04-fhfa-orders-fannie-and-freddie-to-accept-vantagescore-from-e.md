detail: "The Federal Housing Finance Agency has told Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to approve all mortgage lenders to use VantageScore 4.0, an alternative to the Classic FICO score that has anchored mortgage underwriting for decades. FHFA Director Bill Pulte framed the move as ending a FICO monopoly, saying the credit bureau has raised its per-score price sharply since 2020.\n\nAn analysis from Keefe, Bruyette & Woods found VantageScore loan volume remains low overall but spiked to 4.4% of total volume in July, concentrated almost entirely among Rocket and United Wholesale Mortgage. The firm said lenders are treating VantageScore mainly as a way to broaden who can qualify for a mortgage rather than a way to cut costs, so most are still pulling FICO scores for the bulk of loans.\n\nSeparately, the Mortgage Bankers Association is pushing to replace the standard three-bureau credit pull with a single-file model it says would lower costs, an idea FICO and some consumer advocates have resisted on the grounds that it could hide errors across bureaus.\n\nVantageScore incorporates rental payment history more readily than Classic FICO in some configurations, a detail lenders and borrowers with thinner credit files have flagged as relevant to qualifying for financing."
why_it_matters: "Investors financing rental purchases with a thinner credit file, including newer landlords, now have a real shot at more lenders accepting VantageScore instead of requiring Classic FICO, which can change who qualifies and at what rate."
