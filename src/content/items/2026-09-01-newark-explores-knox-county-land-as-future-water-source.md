title: "Newark Explores Knox County Land as Future Water Source"
summary: "Newark is evaluating a 300-acre property just north of Utica in Knox County as a potential future water source, either as a well field, a reservoir, or both, though officials say there is no immediate shortage. City council will consider a resolution on September 8 authorizing an 18-month, $180,000 to $200,000 study contract with the landowner that would give Newark first right to buy the property if it proves viable. Officials say a reservoir there could eventually benefit Utica and nearby townships as well as Newark, which currently draws water from the North Fork of the Licking River."
detail: "Newark Water Administrator Brandon Fox told The Reporting Project the city is looking at a 300-acre property in Morgan Township, just north of Utica in Knox County, as a possible long-term water source, while stressing the city currently has plenty of water. A well field or reservoir on the site could feed the North Fork of the Licking River, from which Newark's treatment plant draws, or be piped directly to the plant. Newark City Council's Service Committee is considering a resolution, set for a full council vote on September 8, authorizing Public Service Director David Rhodes to sign an 18-month contract with the landowner to study the property's viability; the geological and hydrological study would cost $180,000 to $200,000, funded by the water department. The contract would give Newark first right to purchase the land if it proves out, or to walk away if it does not. Fox, who previously worked on Columbus's Utilities Department reservoir project in Delaware County, said any eventual construction would require the city to issue water bonds repaid through water department billing. Fox said the city currently treats 7 to 8 million gallons a day against a 15 million gallon daily capacity, and that the study is meant to secure supply years ahead of actual need."
why_it_matters: "Any reservoir or well field that results from this study would ultimately be funded through Newark water bonds repaid by ratepayers, so Licking County landlords should expect the eventual cost, if any, to show up as a water rate increase years from now rather than a tax bill."
