summary: "HUD unveiled a proposed rule that would let local public housing agencies require able-bodied adults on federal housing assistance, including Section 8 vouchers, to work up to 40 hours a week and cap their time in the program at two years or more. Seniors, children and people with disabilities would be exempt. HUD Secretary Scott Turner announced the proposal alongside officials from housing authorities in Champaign County and Vermilion, Illinois, the first two agencies to join a new HUD coalition promoting work-focused policies."
detail: "HUD's proposed rule would give local public housing agencies the option to require able-bodied adults receiving housing assistance, including Section 8 housing choice vouchers, to work up to 40 hours a week and would cap their time in the program at two years or more. Seniors, children and people with disabilities would be excluded. HUD Secretary Scott Turner announced the proposal with officials from the Housing Authority of Champaign County and the Vermilion Housing Authority in Illinois, the first two agencies to join HUD's new Work and Dignity Coalition, which encourages housing authorities to adopt similar policies. Turner cited federal data showing nearly half of non-elderly, able-bodied assisted households lacked a working adult in 2024, and said average tenure in major rental assistance programs has grown to eight or nine years. HUD estimates the change could increase national income by up to $51 million. The rule now enters the federal rulemaking process and must undergo a public comment period before any housing authority, including Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority, could choose to adopt it. According to WIXY.com, the announcement took place in Champaign, Illinois."
why_it_matters: "If HUD finalizes the rule, Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority could eventually opt into work requirements and time limits for voucher holders, changing tenant eligibility and turnover for landlords who accept Section 8 tenants."
