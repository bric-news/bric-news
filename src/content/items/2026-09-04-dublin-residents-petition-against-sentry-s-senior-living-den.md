title: "Dublin Residents Petition Against Sentry's Senior Living Density"
summary: "Neighbors near a proposed senior living development at Bright Road and Emerald Parkway in Dublin have collected more than 100 signatures opposing the project's size and density. Sentry Land Company's concept calls for roughly 175 senior apartments and 19 villas on 17.6 acres, with a main building that city staff calculations show would exceed the density allowed under the site's neighborhood office land-use designation in the Envision Dublin plan."
detail: "Sentry Land Company presented an updated concept for a senior living community in Dublin to the city's Planning and Zoning Commission in August, proposing roughly 175 independent living, assisted living and memory care apartments plus 19 single-story villas on 17.6 acres near Bright Road and Emerald Parkway, next to Hopewell Elementary School. City staff calculated the proposed 207,514-square-foot main building at about 18,000 square feet per acre on the portion of the site zoned neighborhood office, well above the roughly 9,500 square feet per acre the Envision Dublin plan anticipates for that designation, and taller than the one- to two-story buildings the plan and the 2013 Bright Road Plan call for. Nearby homeowners, who started a petition that has drawn more than 100 signatures, cited concerns about the building's height, traffic at the Bright Road and Emerald Parkway entrances, and the loss of the area's rural residential character. Sentry has said the project addresses a documented need for senior housing in Dublin, though city staff noted the developer's cited figure overstated the study's specific senior-unit projection. The concept plan review is nonbinding; Dublin has not received a formal rezoning application, and a full traffic impact study would only be required if the project advances to that stage."
why_it_matters: "The density and height mismatch the city itself flagged, roughly double what Envision Dublin allows on that site, is the kind of gap Dublin has enforced against developers before, so investors eyeing similar-scale senior or multifamily projects in the same corridor should expect the same zoning fight."
