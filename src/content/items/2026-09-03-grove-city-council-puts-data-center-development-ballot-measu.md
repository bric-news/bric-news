title: "Grove City Council puts data center development ballot measure on Nov. 3 election"
summary: "Grove City Council voted unanimously to place the citizen-led Community Consent Amendment on the November 3 ballot, after rejecting a compromise version negotiated between council members and Protect Grove City organizers. The amendment would require a citywide vote before any large-scale development can move forward in the city, and grew out of resident pushback against proposed data center projects, though the measure's language does not name data centers specifically."
detail: "Grove City Council held a special meeting to decide the fate of a citizen petition known as the Community Consent Amendment, which would require voter approval before any large-scale development project could proceed in the city. The petition grew out of resident opposition to proposed data center projects, organized under the group Protect Grove City, though the amendment's own language applies to large development generally rather than naming data centers.\n\nCouncil first considered a negotiated compromise version, worked out between Protect Grove City leadership, the city law director, and some council members, that included concessions on how existing development, expansions, monitoring, and fines would be handled. Council rejected that compromise, with council president Ted Berry saying the public had not seen the new language and that the roughly 1,600 signatures collected on the original petition entitled that version to go to the ballot.\n\nCouncil then voted unanimously to place the original Community Consent Amendment on the November 3 general election ballot, with several members saying they felt legally obligated to do so once the petition qualified, even though they personally opposed it. The council separately passed a resolution, also unanimous, putting members on record against the amendment, arguing it would affect churches, parks, businesses, and residential development citywide and limit the city's ability to provide services. Protect Grove City organizers are continuing to campaign for the amendment's passage."
why_it_matters: "A developer or investor with a large project planned in Grove City should factor in that any big development, not just data centers, could require a citywide vote starting this fall if the Community Consent Amendment passes on November 3."
