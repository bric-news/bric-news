title: "Grove City Sends Data Center Ballot Measure to November Vote"
summary: "Grove City Council voted unanimously to put a citizen-petitioned charter amendment, the Community Consent Amendment, on the November 3 ballot. The amendment would require a citywide vote before any large-scale development moves forward, a response to pushback over proposed data center projects even though the amendment does not name data centers specifically. Council rejected a compromise version negotiated between organizers and the city's law director, and separately passed a resolution putting members on record against the amendment."
detail: "Grove City Council held a special meeting and voted on three measures affecting the city's charter. Council first rejected, without a vote, a compromise amendment worked out between Protect Grove City organizer Jen Belt, the city law director, and a council member, which organizers said would have addressed development, expansion, monitoring, and fines. Council President Ted Berry said the compromise had not been seen by the roughly 1,600 residents who signed the original petition, so the original version had to go to voters. Council then voted unanimously to place the original Community Consent Amendment, which requires a citywide vote before any large-scale development project can proceed, on the November 3 general election ballot. The amendment does not use the term data centers, but growing opposition to proposed data center projects in Grove City drove the petition campaign. Council separately passed a resolution putting its members on record opposing the amendment, citing potential effects on churches, parks, businesses, and residential development, as well as the city's ability to provide services. Council members said they felt obligated to put the measure on the ballot because it had the required signatures, even though most opposed it."
why_it_matters: "If voters approve the amendment in November, any large development project in Grove City, not just data centers, would need a citywide vote before it could proceed, adding a new approval step and timeline risk for investors with land or projects planned there."
