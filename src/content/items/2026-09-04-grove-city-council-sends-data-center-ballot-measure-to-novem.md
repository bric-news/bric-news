title: "Grove City Council Sends Data Center Ballot Measure to November Vote"
summary: "Grove City Council voted unanimously to place the citizen-led Community Consent Amendment, which would require voter approval before large-scale development projects can proceed, on the November 3 ballot. Council first rejected a compromise version negotiated between city officials and the Protect Grove City organizers, then approved putting the original petition version to voters after finding it had met signature requirements. Council separately passed a resolution opposing the amendment, citing potential effects on churches, parks, businesses and residential development."
detail: "Grove City Council held a special meeting to resolve the fate of the Community Consent Amendment, a citizen petition that would require voter approval before any large-scale development moves forward in the city. Council first considered a compromise amendment worked out between city officials, a council member and Protect Grove City organizer Jen Belt, but rejected it before a vote because members said the public had not seen its terms. Council then voted unanimously to place the original petition on the ballot, with Council President Ted Berry saying members felt a constitutional responsibility to do so once the petition met its signature threshold, even though he said most members oppose it. Council also unanimously passed a resolution formally opposing the amendment, citing potential harm to churches, parks, businesses, residential development and the city's ability to fund services. While the amendment's language does not name data centers, growing opposition to proposed data center projects in Grove City drove the original petition campaign. Voters will decide the measure's fate in the November 3 general election."
why_it_matters: "If Grove City voters approve the amendment, any landlord or developer with a large project planned there, not just a data center, would need voter sign-off first, a template other Franklin County suburbs fighting similar projects are likely to watch."
