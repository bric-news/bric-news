title: "The Real Cost of Self-Managing a Columbus Rental Versus Hiring a Manager"
summary: "RL Property Management laid out the full cost comparison between self-managing a Columbus rental and hiring a manager, current as of September 2026. Professional flat-rate management runs $117 to $184 per unit per month, or roughly $1,529 to $2,333 a year on a $1,400 rental. Self-managing carries lower fees but shifts costs into time, vacancy days and retail repair pricing. Columbus's new rental registry, open October 1 through December 31 at $15 per unit with no exemptions, adds another compliance task regardless of which path an owner chooses."
detail: "RL Property Management built out the Columbus math for owners weighing self-management against hiring a manager. On a $1,400 rental, its flat-rate plans run $1,529 a year on the Passive tier, $1,769 on Standard, or $2,333 on Premium, each including a $125 accounting fee and no separate leasing fee. Self-managing looks cheaper on paper because it avoids those fees, but the company says the real cost shows up elsewhere: in vacancy days, in paying retail prices for repairs instead of a manager's contractor rates, and in compliance exposure.\n\nTwo specific numbers anchor the comparison. First, a well-priced Columbus single-family rental leases in 14 to 21 days once rent-ready, and each vacant day on a $1,400 unit costs about $46, so a saved week of vacancy is worth roughly $322. Second, Columbus's new rental registry opens for filing Oct. 1 and closes Dec. 31, 2026, at $15 per unit annually, with no exemption for single-family homes and no phase-in by portfolio size, meaning every owner needs to budget for the filing whether they self-manage or not.\n\nThe company also notes that a Franklin County eviction filing costs $149 as of Aug. 1, 2026, and roughly $448 all in when handled through RL Property Management, versus the larger cost of the six weeks of lost rent an eviction typically represents. RL Property Management frames the choice as one that depends on turnover rate: flat-rate management tends to win in years a unit turns over, while percentage-based pricing can win during a long, quiet tenancy."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus owner who has not run this math since before the rental registry took effect should rerun it now, since the $15 per unit filing fee and the Oct. 1 deadline apply the same to a self-managed duplex as to a professionally managed one."
