title: "Court Orders Columbus Apartment Complex Closed Over Squatters, Drugs"
summary: "A Franklin County Environmental Court judge ordered Broadway Court Apartments on West Broad Street closed by Sept. 15 after the Columbus City Attorney's Property Action Team obtained a preliminary injunction citing years of code violations, deteriorating conditions and a police raid that found suspected fentanyl, crack cocaine and methamphetamine along with 16 people occupying units that were supposed to be vacant. The five units with known tenants must vacate by Sept. 14."
detail: "Columbus City Attorney Zach Klein announced that the city's Property Action Team secured a preliminary injunction from Franklin County Environmental Court Judge Stephanie Mingo requiring the closure of Broadway Court Apartments, at 2009-2033 W. Broad St., no later than Sept. 15. Five units with known tenants must vacate by Sept. 14, and a permanent injunction hearing is scheduled for Sept. 28.\n\nThe Property Action Team first filed a case against the property's owner, 2031 Broad LLC, which acquired the complex in February, back in June, after which the court ordered the owner to fix code violations, including damaged and unsecured doors and windows, deteriorating exterior components, damaged lighting, and deteriorating stairways, by early September. The city said the violations went unaddressed.\n\nThe closure followed a Friday incident in which someone jumped from a third story window as Columbus police responded to a report at a supposedly vacant unit. Officers searched two units and found suspected fentanyl, crack cocaine and methamphetamine, along with 16 people occupying units that were supposed to be empty; four people with outstanding warrants were arrested. City Attorney Klein said property owners who leave buildings unsecured and let code violations go unaddressed can expect the city to pursue every legal tool available, including a full property closure."
why_it_matters: "This is the kind of enforcement outcome the city's Property Action Team can pursue when a landlord ignores a code violation order, so an owner who receives a similar notice from Columbus should treat the deadline in it as the last practical chance to fix the problem before a judge orders the property closed."
