detail: "Newark is exploring whether a 300-acre property just north of Utica in Knox County could become a future water source for the city and, potentially, surrounding communities. Water Administrator Brandon Fox told The Reporting Project the city continually evaluates long-term water sources and sees potential in the Morgan Township site, either as a reservoir that could release water into the North Fork of the Licking River or a well field piped directly to the Newark Water Plant.\n\nThe full Newark City Council was set to consider a resolution at its September 8 meeting authorizing an 18-month contract with the landowner for a geological and hydrological study, expected to cost $180,000 to $200,000 and covered by water department reserve funds. The contract would give the city first right to purchase the property if it proves viable, or to walk away if it does not.\n\nFox said the city currently draws most of its water from the North Fork of the Licking River, with capacity to treat up to 15 million gallons a day against current demand of 7 to 8 million gallons, plus a 100-acre backup wellfield near St. Louisville. He said a future decision to build a reservoir or drill wells would likely require the city to issue water bonds, similar to how Columbus financed its Delaware County reservoir in the 1990s and 2000s, but on a smaller scale."
